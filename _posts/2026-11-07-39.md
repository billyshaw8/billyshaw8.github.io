心力衰竭（心衰）患者突然出现晕厥，属于非常严重的高危预警信号，必须立即就医诊治。
当心衰患者发生晕厥时，根本原因在于心脏泵血功能突然骤降或中断，导致脑部供血瞬间不足（脑缺血缺氧）。
一、 心衰导致突然晕厥的主要原因
1. 恶性心律失常（最常见、最危险）
心衰患者的心肌结构常有改变（如心室扩大、心肌纤维化），极易引发严重的心律失常：
* 室性心动过缓或房室传导阻滞：心脏跳动过慢，甚至出现长间歇，心脏无法及时将血液泵送至大脑。
* 室性心动过速（室速）或心室颤动（室颤）：心脏处于无效的快速震颤状态，失去正常的泵血能力，脑血流瞬间中断。
2. 急性心输出量骤降（泵衰竭）
在重度心衰或病情急性加重时，左心室射血分数（LVEF）极低，或者在身体活动、姿势改变时，心脏无法满足机体的血液需求，导致血压骤降、脑供血不足。
3. 药物引起的体位性低血压
心衰患者通常需要长期服用多种药物（如利尿剂、血管紧张素受体脑啡肽酶抑制剂 ARNI、ACEI/ARB、β受体阻滞剂等）。
* 利尿过度可能导致血容量不足；
* 降压或扩血管药物可能导致在突然站立或体位改变时出现直立性低血压，进而引发晕厥。
4. 合并急性栓塞（如肺栓塞）
心衰患者血液处于高凝状态，若下肢深静脉形成血栓并脱落导致急性肺栓塞，会引发严重的右心衰竭和急性循环衰竭，表现为突发晕厥。
二、 现场突发晕厥的急救处理
如果身边有心衰患者突发晕厥，请按以下步骤紧急处置：
1. 保持平卧与呼吸通畅：立即让患者平卧，解开衣领，头偏向一侧，防止呕吐物窒息。
2. 检查意识与呼吸：
    * 大声呼唤患者并轻拍双肩；
    * 观察胸廓是否有起伏（观察时间约 5～10 秒）。
3. 立即拨打急救电话：
    * 若无意识且无正常呼吸（或仅有濒死喘息），立即拨打当地急救电话，并立刻开始进行心肺复苏（CPR）；若现场有自动体外除颤器（AED），应尽快取用。
    * 若患者很快恢复意识，也不可自行走动或回家休息，必须就地平卧等待救护车或送往医院检查。
三、 医院后续重点检查与治疗
入院后，医生通常会进行以下评估，以明确晕厥原因并预防猝死：
* 动态心电图（24-72小时 Holter / 植入式心电记录器）：捕捉隐匿的恶性心律失常。
* 超声心动图：评估心功能状态及瓣膜情况。
* 电解质与血药浓度监测：排除低钾、低镁及药物毒性（如地高辛中毒）。
* 评估评估植入式心脏复律除颤器（ICD）：对于合并恶性心律失常或重度心衰高危猝死风险的患者，ICD 是预防心源性猝死最有效的手段。


Sudden fainting (syncope) in a patient with heart failure is a critical warning sign that requires immediate medical attention.
When a heart failure patient faints, the core underlying issue is a sudden drop or temporary pause in cardiac output, leading to acute cerebral hypoperfusion (insufficient blood flow to the brain).
Key Causes of Syncope in Heart Failure
1. Malignant Arrhythmias (Most Common & Dangerous)
Heart failure causes structural changes in the heart muscle (such as ventricular enlargement or fibrosis), making it highly susceptible to lethal heart rhythms:
* Severe Bradycardia or Heart Block: The heart beats far too slowly or pauses, preventing adequate blood from reaching the brain.
* Ventricular Tachycardia (VT) or Ventricular Fibrillation (VF): The heart beats erratically or quivers uselessly, causing blood pressure and brain perfusion to drop to zero within seconds.
2. Acute Drop in Cardiac Output (Pump Failure)
In advanced heart failure or during acute decompensation, the left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) is severely compromised. Sudden exertion or position changes can overwhelm the heart's ability to supply enough blood, triggering a sharp drop in blood pressure.
3. Drug-Induced Orthostatic Hypotension
Heart failure regimens often involve medications such as diuretics, ARNI/ACEi/ARBs, and beta-blockers:
* Over-diuresis can lead to hypovolemia (low blood volume).
* Vasodilators and antihypertensives can cause a sudden, severe drop in blood pressure upon standing (postural/orthostatic hypotension).
4. Co-occurring Acute Embolism (e.g., Pulmonary Embolism)
Heart failure increases the risk of blood clots. If a deep vein thrombosis (DVT) travels to the lungs (Pulmonary Embolism), it can obstruct pulmonary blood flow, cause acute right-heart failure, and lead to sudden syncope.
Immediate First Aid Protocol
If someone with heart failure faints in your presence:
1. Position the Person Safely: Lay them flat on their back and elevate their legs slightly (if possible). Loosen tight clothing around the neck. Turn their head to the side to keep the airway clear if they vomit.
2. Check Consciousness and Breathing:
    * Call their name loudly and tap their shoulders firmly.
    * Observe their chest for normal breathing (take no more than 5–10 seconds).
3. Call Emergency Services Immediately:
    * If unresponsive and not breathing normally (or only gasping): Call emergency services immediately (e.g., 000 in Australia, 911 in the US) and start CPR. Send someone to locate an AED (Automated External Defibrillator) if available.
    * If they regain consciousness quickly: Do not allow them to sit up suddenly, walk around, or go home. Keep them resting flat until medical professionals arrive.
Medical Evaluation & Interventions
At the hospital, diagnostic tests will focus on determining the precise cause and preventing sudden cardiac death (SCD):
* Continuous / Ambulatory ECG Monitoring (Holter or Implantable Loop Recorder): To detect transient or hidden arrhythmias.
* Echocardiogram (Cardiac Ultrasound): To assess current ejection fraction, heart structure, and valve function.
* Blood Panels & Electrolytes: To check for low potassium or magnesium levels, as well as potential medication toxicity (e.g., Digoxin).
* Evaluation for an ICD (Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator): For patients at high risk of sudden cardiac death due to severe heart failure or documented malignant arrhythmias, an ICD is often recommended to deliver life-saving shocks if a lethal rhythm occurs.


During or immediately after fainting (syncope), a person typically does not feel pain while they are unconscious, because the brain briefly loses consciousness due to the lack of blood flow.
However, pain can be involved in a few specific ways:
1. Pain Before Fainting (Warning Signs)
Depending on what caused the cardiac issue or fainting episode, a person might experience pain right before losing consciousness:
* Chest Pain or Pressure: If the heart failure decompensation or arrhythmia is triggered by an acute event like a heart attack (myocardial infarction) or severe angina, the person may feel chest tightness, pain radiating to the jaw, neck, or left arm, or severe pressure.
* Severe Shortness of Breath: Sudden accumulation of fluid in the lungs (acute pulmonary edema) can cause intense distress and chest discomfort before fainting.
2. Pain After Fainting (Injuries from the Fall)
Because syncope happens very suddenly, the individual often collapses without being able to catch themselves. Common causes of pain after waking up include:
* Traumatic Injuries: Head impacts, concussions, facial cuts, or fractures (e.g., hips, wrists, ribs) caused by the sudden fall.
* Muscle Aches: Soft tissue bruising from hitting the floor or furniture.
3. Chest Pain or Soreness Post-Resuscitation
If the fainting was caused by a cardiac arrest or severe arrhythmia that required CPR (chest compressions) or electrical defibrillation (AED shocks), the patient will likely experience significant chest soreness or rib fractures after being revived.
Important Safety Note: If someone experiences chest pain, pressure, or shortness of breath prior to fainting, or if they sustained a head injury during a fall, this indicates an urgent medical emergency. Emergency services should be contacted immediately.
